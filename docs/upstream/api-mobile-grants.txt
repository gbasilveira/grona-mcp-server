Mobile grants API
A grant lets one identified shopper see one money-bearing offer in your app, such as 10% off this order. Grona signs each grant, the Grona SDK shows an offer only when it holds a matching grant, and your order backend checks the grant again before it applies the treatment. This page covers the one call your backend makes to fetch grants, where it fits, and how to handle failures and caching. The mobile grants API is available on request. It is not part of Grona Embed.
Where it fits
Section titled “Where it fits”
The app identifies the shopper.
It passes an opaque customer id, such as
cus_7Hq2Lm9x
, to the SDK’s
identify
. See
Shoppers and identity
.
The SDK asks the app for grants.
It does this on
identify
, then again shortly before the nearest grant expires, and at least once an hour, through the grant source your app provides. Your app passes the request to your backend, inside the shopper’s signed-in session.
Your backend calls Grona.
It sends
POST https://api.grona.ai/v1/mobile/grants
with your Grona secret key and the customer id from the shopper’s session.
Your backend returns the grants to the app.
The SDK verifies each one and shows an offer only when a grant matches its campaign, variant, treatment type and value.
Your order backend verifies the grant at order time.
It checks the signature and claims, enforces single use and applies the treatment. See
Grants and order verification
.

Repeat calls
Section titled “Repeat calls”
Repeat calls for the same customer return that customer’s currently valid grants. Grona does not mint a new grant for a campaign while an unexpired one exists for that customer, so retries and refetches cannot multiply the grants a shopper can redeem. That makes the call safe to retry.
Your order backend still enforces single use for each grant, keyed on its
jti
claim. Once a grant expires, a later call can return a new grant for the same campaign, with a new
jti
. If an offer must apply once per customer rather than once per grant, your order backend enforces that too.
Caution
Take
customerId
from the shopper’s authenticated session on your backend. If your route accepted a customer id from the app, anyone could fetch grants for another shopper.
The customer id
Section titled “The customer id”
customerId
is the same opaque id your app passes to the SDK’s
identify
. It becomes the grant’s
sub
claim, and both the SDK and your order backend check it against the shopper. Never use an email address, a phone number or a national id.
The grant format
Section titled “The grant format”
Each grant is a compact JWS signed by Grona, at most 4 KB, that names one shopper, one campaign and variant, and one treatment. It expires at most 24 hours after it is issued.
Grants and order verification
lists the header and every claim.

When the call to Grona fails or times out, tell the app the fetch failed rather than returning an empty list. The app’s grant source then reports a failure (on Android,
fetchGrants
returns
null
), and the SDK retries with backoff. An empty list means the fetch succeeded and no grant applies, so the SDK does not retry straight away. Either way the shopper sees no offer until a grant arrives: the campaign gives way to the next one in that placement and the app keeps working. The SDK waits at most 10 seconds for a grant fetch, so keep your route fast.
Caching
Section titled “Caching”
Grants are short-lived: each one expires at most 24 hours after it is issued. The SDK keeps them in memory only, and refetches shortly before the nearest one expires and at least once an hour.
You do not need to cache grants. Fetching them on each request from the app is the expected pattern: repeat calls return the customer’s currently valid grants, not new ones.
If you do cache, key the cache by customer id, never share entries between shoppers, and drop an entry no later than the earliest
exp
among its grants.
Clear any cached grants when the shopper signs out.
What Grona does not provide
Section titled “What Grona does not provide”
No redeem endpoint.
Your order backend verifies each grant itself and enforces single use, keyed on the grant’s
jti
claim and never on the token string. See
Grants and order verification
.
No key-download endpoint.
There is no API for fetching signing keys. See
Rotate signing keys
.
